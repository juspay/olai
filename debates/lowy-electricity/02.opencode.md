# Round 2 — opencode (module): name the voltage before we name the manifest

Two strong openings to answer, and one of them corrects me. I verified the disputed claims against the code before writing this; where the verification went against my own round-1 numbers I say so first, because a module stance that cannot survive a `git log` is no stance at all.

## The correction I owe grok: the store's plug has moved, and that is my point, not his

grok's factual claim stands up. `git log --oneline -- packages/store/src/index.ts` returns five edits — #58 (the first live loop), #59, #63 (`commit`), #81 (`outlines` as a collection), #112 (`Codec.unreadable`). My round 1 leaned on `packages/store/src/disk.ts`'s three edits and called the socket disciplined; the *public face* is `index.ts`, and it has not been still. Conceded, and the concession sharpens rather than dulls the module case.

Here is why. The five edits to `index.ts` are exactly what a healthy receptacle's history looks like in its first year: **the socket moving because the volatility behind it demanded a better socket** — `commit` arriving because the write gate was learned, the collection re-keying because the wire outgrew a stream, `unreadable` because a mounted directory that vanishes is different news from a file that fails to parse. Those are not shrapnel events. They are the receptacle being *fitted*. And critically, every one of them landed **behind the workspace boundary** — `server`, `chat`, `web`, `tests` consume `Store.make({ root, codec })` and were re-pointed by the compiler, no version pin, no manifests republished. That is the module delivering the vault *while the plug is still being invented*. grok frames the same fact as "the plug is not ready to leave the house" and says no own-repo this cycle. We agree on the act; I disagree that the fact argues against fable's candidate forever. It argues for *fable's candidate as a graduation with a date*, not an extraction with none — which is what I signed in round 1 and still sign.

Where I push back on the framing: grok's own kitchen-sinks citation — "isolation selects for stability" (`conclusion.md:31`) — cuts both ways. Counting `index.ts` edits to prove the plug is moving is *still selecting on the socket's motion*, and both fable and the article have the right answer to that: "a receptacle is *supposed* to look stable from the outside" is the end state, not the disqualification, and Löwy's question is "what churns behind it." The honest verdict from the log is: behind the socket, the churn is real (`store.ts:82-87` spanning refused probes, the settle/backstop pair, the prune); at the socket, the churn is *slowing* (#112 was deepened error scope, not a re-plug). That is a receptacle approaching its mature shape in the open, inside the package that feeds it. It does not need a manifest to be a vault. It has one consumer today; the manifest waits for the second, dated.

## Steelmanning fable, then the module reading of the same files

fable's strongest move is refusing to let "no second consumer" be the bar, and I will not re-litigate it — the article says reuse is a consequence ("using power in one house indistinguishable from another"), and `@kolu/surface` itself was extracted at one consumer. If the charter forced me to pick a package candidate, `@olai/store` is it, as I said in round 1.

fable's *markdown* re-argument is where the altitude question is sharpest, and I want to answer it precisely because fable states the concession fairly: "I expect opencode to argue the module and I will sign that as the floor." Here is the module, argued — but note first that fable and grok are talking about **two different files**, and the debate only makes sense if we keep them apart:

- At package altitude, fable is proposing the *rendering* socket: markdown in, sanitized HTML out, with `pipeline.ts`'s plugin order, `sanitise.ts`'s allowlist, `render.ts`'s per-block footnote re-minting. Its four consumers are notes, documents, titles, chat replies — all client-side.
- At upstream altitude, grok's `buildSurfaceClient` candidate would absorb the *chunk* socket: `packages/web/src/markdown.ts` (109 lines), the second `Bun.build` that exists "because the helper does not" (`markdown.ts:12-19`) and that would "delete most of itself" given a `splitting?: boolean` on the helper.

The fact that the same word "markdown" names a candidate at two altitudes is the strongest evidence that **the vault is the directory `web/src/client/markdown/`** — fourteen files, one public entry through `render.ts`'s memo — and the extraction question is which of its two *faces* graduates: the render pipeline that is genuinely olai (and stays, on grok's own reasoning that notes and documents *are* the domain), or the build step that is genuinely kolu (and is upstream's to complete). The module reading dissolves the apparent fable/grok collision: the rendering receptacle stays where it is as a directory whose socket is the one-pipeline rule fable names (already enforced by the chunk boundary — `pipeline.ts:18-19`, "an `import()` whose specifier is a variable is left alone by the bundler"), and the *chunk* receptacle is exactly what grok's candidate 1 pays. No `@olai/markdown` manifest is needed for either act. fable gets the encapsulation; grok gets the upstream completion; the manifest tax is paid by nobody.

I also owe fable one narrow rebuttal on the "stability disqualifies" charge: I did not argue the pipeline is disqualified because stable. The prior debate deferred it for want of a dated second consumer, and this round's evidence says the stronger thing — its two faces belong to two different owners, which is why a package would weld them. Welding is the thing the Edit union's own header warns against (`surface/src/edit.ts:23-29`, "five spellings of one list"). Not stability.

## Steelmanning grok, then signing his candidates — with the module half named

grok's two candidates are the best real extraction proposals on the table, and I verified them line by line:

- **`buildSurfaceClient`:** the `splitting: false` is hardcoded in the actual `Bun.build` call (`node_modules/@kolu/surface-app/src/bun.ts:149`), not exposed on the helper's options; `freshStaticLayer` does negotiate `br`/`zstd`/`gzip` under the hashed prefix and refuses the shell (server.ts, confirmed); olai composes the two re-derived post-steps at `build.ts:215-232` (`buildMarkdownChunk` then `precompressAssets`). The litany grok writes — `.br`/`.gz` but never `.zst` while the server prefers `zstd` in between (`precompress.ts:41-42`), quality 11 vs level 9 vs "did it beat identity" (`precompress.ts:70-84`), the sibling-skip and the dev-tax — is real and it is *wire-physics*, the same category as the frame cap that `listener.ts` dropped ("removing the knob", `listener.ts:35-40` per grok, and `docs/architecture.md:79` records the same pattern for the listener). This is an upstream receptacle. I sign it.

- **The `connectSurface` readout:** grok's candidate 2 is, at its core, the observation that a receptacle is not allowed to read 110 or 220 depending on whether you brought a voltmeter (`01.grok.md:66`), and that is exactly right. The oscilloscope today is concrete in `Indicator.tsx:61-84`: `client.health()` walks every enrolled sub and allocates a record per sub, enrolment is per *key* (one per open document, one per transcript row), it re-folds on every membership change, and six JSX expressions would each compile to their own walk without the memo with the `equals` on names. That is measuring frequency by hand. I sign the upstream payment: `status` must not say `live` over an errored subscription; pending does not degrade; the framework owns whether green can lie.

And here the module altitude delivers its load-bearing half of the convergence, because grok's own split requires it: **the framework owns the *fact*; olai's `packages/web/src/client/connection/status.ts` owns the *policy*.** grok says this file "shrinks to chrome over a five-state readout; it does not disappear" (`01.grok.md:92`). Correct — and it is my named module candidate from round 1's ledger (M2's sibling). What stays in `status.ts` after the upstream payment is the part no framework may own: that olai's green claim is "the files on disk reach this page" (`Indicator.tsx:100-101`), that `retired` reads as "server restarted" with the reload spelled out (`status.ts:61-66`), that pending is what every page load looks like (`status.ts:91-99`). Those are domain sentences — not outlines/days/marks domain, but *olai*-domain. The receptacle decomposition here is a two-storey vault: kolu owns the five states and their truth, olai owns the names and the LOOK table, and the socket between them is one accessor. That is the article's pattern at *two* altitudes for one axis, which is I think the cleanest thing this debate has produced so far.

On `keysBus`: grok is right that it is a fix, not a vault (`01.grok.md:72`). It is shrapnel the existing wire socket throws; the vault is already installed, it just leaks. Ledger it as a fix.
