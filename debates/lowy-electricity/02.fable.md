# Round 2 — fable

Both openings did something the kitchen-sinks debate never quite did: they argued *for* their receptacles with the litany written out, and the two of them converged on a finding neither the survey nor the first debate produced — the connection readout (opencode's M2, grok's candidate 2) as one axis seen from two altitudes. That convergence is the round's headline and I sign it below. First, what each opening does to my altitude.

## grok's vault-vs-incorporation distinction beats my candidate 1 as I stated it

The sharpest sentence in round 1 is grok's: *"Installing the vault does not wait. Incorporating the power company is not the vault."* That distinction is truer to the article than my opening was. The article demands the receptacle in the wall — it says nothing about the power company's corporate structure, and my "store graduates to its own repo" conflated the two exactly as grok charges. The evidence is also against me on timing: the plug moved in #112 (`Codec.unreadable`), which makes it one cycle old — "do not ship a moving plug" is not deferral-by-reflex, it is the same rule that kept `@kolu/surface` inside kolu until its socket had aged at one consumer.

What I salvage, and it is worth salvaging precisely: **the recognition with a dated trigger.** The store's vault exists (all three stances now say so — opencode's M1 names `disk.ts` as its heart, grok's "what I will not graduate" section writes the store's litany better than my opening did). The incorporation should carry its trigger in the ledger rather than a vague "later": *the plug survives two consecutive release cycles unedited AND a second FileSystem face (the worker/browser layer its own dev-dependency note anticipates) lands against the unchanged socket.* When both are true, the manifest is laid on a proven socket — opencode's "promotion, not extraction." Until then, the package *is* the receptacle, which is grok's row 4 and I sign it.

## Markdown: grok's domain-test counter lands; the package dies at my own altitude

Grok's one-line kill: *"Markdown is how notes and documents are read — closer to the domain than electricity."* That is the article's second reason turned against my candidate, and it is right in a way the first debate's "no second consumer" never was. In olai, markdown is not the power coming into the house — it is the *language the family speaks at dinner*. The rendering machinery (sanitizer schema, plugin order, grammar set) is genuinely non-domain, but the socket it feeds — "what a note means when drawn" — is olai's own subject matter. The receptacle exists and is correctly a *directory with a one-pipeline invariant*; no manifest would move the domain line. Conceded at package altitude, permanently this time, and on better grounds than last debate's.

So my altitude's honest yield this round: **zero new manifests, one recognition, one dated promotion trigger.** I consider that a result, not a defeat — the charter asked what the bar demands, and at package altitude the bar demands patience with two vaults that already exist.

## Against opencode's doctrine: the module socket has a tax too, and naming it makes M1–M4 stronger

opencode's doctrine — "the import list *is* the socket, compiled" — is four-fifths true and the missing fifth matters. Between packages, the direction is machine-checked: `bun install` fails on an undeclared edge, and a browser bundle physically cannot reach `node:child_process` through the workspace graph. *Within* a package, nothing checks. `wire.ts:10`'s "this is the only file in the client that knows a websocket exists" is enforced by discipline, not by a compiler — any new client file may import `connectSurface` tomorrow and the build stays green. The markdown directory's olai-boundary (only `rewrite.ts`/`tags.ts` reach format/surface/testids) is the same: convention. The prior debate's six-import drift is opencode's own exhibit that hand-kept boundary claims rot — and a module-altitude receptacle's boundary claim is *exactly* a hand-kept claim.

This does not defeat the module altitude; it prices it. The module receptacle needs an enforcement story the manifest gets for free, and olai already owns the cheap version: **the socket-claim test** — a unit test that greps the tree for the forbidden import (`connectSurface` outside `wire.ts`, `node:zlib` outside the build, format-imports inside `markdown/` beyond the two named plugins), the same way `clock.test.ts` pins the two-clocks agreement and the WCAG test pins the palette promise. One test per claimed socket, and "one file knows X" stops being prose. I ask opencode to take this as a friendly amendment: M1–M4 each ship with their claim test, and the module altitude's honesty problem is retired.

On the candidates themselves: **M3 is the type specimen, agreed. M4 (the Edit union) I sign wholesale** — it is kitchen-sinks item 5 wearing its correct Lowy clothing, and the litany ("four spellings of volatility behind one closed union") is the best statement of that seam anyone has written. **M1 I sign as the heart of the store's existing vault** (it argues *for* my recognition, not against it). **M2 I sign as the olai-side half** of the readout finding, with grok's split: the framework owns whether green can lie; `status.ts` stays as this house's LOOK policy.

## grok's two upstream candidates: sign both, one condition each

**`buildSurfaceClient` as one socket** — signed. The two `#upstream` tickets ratified as one receptacle is the round's best *practical* outcome: a `precompress` option landing while `splitting` stays hardcoded would leave the oscilloscope in every consumer's hand, and grok's frame-cap precedent (paid upstream by *removing the knob*) is the right discipline. Condition: the ledger entry must name what stays in olai (Solid transform, Tailwind, fonts, icons — "this house's appliances"), so the payment does not creep into taking the whole build.

**The status readout** — signed, and it is the strongest new finding of this debate: *a receptacle that reads 110 or 220 depending on whether you also brought a voltmeter is not a receptacle.* Condition: the "pending does not degrade" rule travels with the payment as a default (grok already argues it is universal), because a framework readout that ambers on every page load would be rejected by every consumer and the payment would bounce.

**`keysbus` as a fix, not a vault** — signed as filed.

## The question for round 3

The ledger is nearly written. What remains genuinely open is one thing: **the store's incorporation trigger** — grok says "not this cycle" without naming the cycle it would say yes to; I have proposed one (plug unedited two cycles + the second FileSystem face lands). opencode proposed "when a second consumer exists, with the date." Round 3 should converge on the trigger's exact wording, because "recorded, not urged" without a trigger is how the first debate's deferrals were left — and the one lesson both debates now agree on is that a deferral without a date is a decision nobody made.
