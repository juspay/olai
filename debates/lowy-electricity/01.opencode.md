# Round 1 — opencode (the MODULE altitude): sockets without manifests

Juval Löwy's electricity is functionality that is never domain-functional: AC or DC, 110 or 220 volts, single phase or three, 50 or 60 hertz, solar, generator, or grid — all of it encapsulated behind an opaque receptacle so that no appliance ever has to know. The charter names one precedent for that receptacle in olai's history: kolu's surface framework, extracted at ONE consumer and aged. The other precedent is the one nobody names: `packages/server/src/listener.ts` used to be a whole sequence — origin gate, upgrade, stale-tab check, heartbeat enrolment, serving stack — kept in step with kolu's own example by hand. It is `serveSurfaceApp` now (`docs/architecture.md:79`). The sequence did not leave as a package first; it was a module that had already stopped knowing, and then kolu took it. **The module is the unit the vault is actually built out of.** A manifest is what a module earns later.

I argue the receptacles olai needs next are files, not packages — one file whose imports are the socket, everything behind them free to churn. The manifest tax is real and the prior debate paid it: `@olai/store`'s own-repo extraction was "properly deferred" for want of a dated second consumer (`debates/kitchen-sinks/conclusion.md:18`). Deferring the manifest must not defer the encapsulation. Below: the module doctrine against the manifest tax, four named receptacles that pass all four of the article's tests, and the candidates I sign at the other two altitudes.

## The doctrine: the vault is a file; the manifest is its history

Löwy's test is the oscilloscope: "you would have to first expose the wires, measure the frequency with an oscilloscope, and certify the voltage with a voltmeter" ("Volatility-Based Decomposition", the unencapsulated-house passage). The oscilloscope is not a build artifact. It is **whatever a consumer must know today that a socket would hide** — and in a TypeScript workspace, that knowledge is measured in *imports*, not in `package.json`s. `packages/web/src/client/wire.ts:32` is the only import of `connectSurface` in the client; the claim on line 10, "This is the only file in the client that knows a websocket exists," is enforced by nothing that a manifest would add. What enforces it is that every other module imports `{ olai, connectionStatus }` from `./wire.ts` and is one type error away from disagreement (`packages/surface/src/index.ts:7` states the same rule at the package seam). A `package.json` boundary is a heavier instrument for a job the import list already does.

The empirical anchor from the last debate is the proof. `@olai/git`'s socket — `packages/git/src/index.ts`, one `open(root)` answering business verbs — has **never been edited since extraction** (`git log --diff-filter=M -- packages/git/src/index.ts` is empty, ratified at `conclusion.md:31`), while `packages/ops/src/pending.ts` above it churned through every git PR in the window. The plumbing/policy axis is where the vault lives, and the vault was built *before* the manifest: `git.ts` was a module with one export surface while it still lived at `ops/src/git.ts` (`docs/architecture.md:25` — "It was `ops/src/git.ts` and moved out"). The extraction did not create the socket; it **promoted** one. Parnas's criterion — decompose on "the need to look for change," not functionality ("Shoulders of Giants" box) — operates on a file when the file's imports are the socket and everything behind them is private. This is the altitude where change is actually tossed: open the file, throw the grenade, close the file; the shrapnel dies at its `export` list.

Against the manifest, then, the charges the prior debate ratified and I carry:

1. **The tax buys enforcement nothing a file lacks.** `surface/src/index.ts:41-45` deleted the one member that answered "who is on the other end," because the framework reserves `system/identity` and an app declaring its own is "declaring a second answer to a question already answered." That was a *module* decision — one file's header — and it holds across every consumer without a boundary bump.

2. **The tax spends review on quarantine, not on the axis.** `conclusion.md:9` ratified fixing stale manifest claims against six real cross-boundary imports — the manifest had drifted from the imports it claimed to gate. The oscilloscope was being measured by a hand-kept mirror and the mirror lied. Modules cannot drift that way: the import list *is* the socket, compiled.

3. **Stability is what the vault produces, not what it is for.** "The survey found the rooms; the debate placed the walls" (`conclusion.md:31`) — isolation selects for stability, and stability is the *inverse* of volatility decomposition. A manifest extracted under the isolation lens arrests a module at its current shape; the module left inside the package keeps churning behind its socket, which is the only way the socket stays honest. A receptacle you plan to delete is not a receptacle (`conclusion.md:15`, engaging the prior verdict by name) — and the module receptacle is not planned for deletion. It is the unit olai deletes *around*.

## The candidates — four tests each

### M1 — `packages/store/src/disk.ts`: the disk, as the probe is allowed to see it

**The supply-side volatility litany (AC/DC/voltage/phase):** platform path separators converted at the boundary so "everything above … reads the same everywhere, with no ambient `process.platform` anywhere" (`disk.ts:11-19`); a walk that PRUNES dot-directories and `node_modules` because "a served directory is a working tree" and git writes to `.git` constantly (`disk.ts:20-22`, `docs/architecture.md:22`); a file vanishing between two syscalls being normal rather than an error — "a probe races every writer on the machine" (`disk.ts:23-26`); `inotify` discarding null filenames (`disk.ts:82`); a rename across file systems being a copy, so staging is same-directory (`disk.ts:31-33`); mtime+size being a deliberately coarse stamp, the trade the design took (`disk.ts:39-43`). That is AC/DC/220/three-phase for "what is under the root."

**The opaque socket:** the `Disk` interface — `listing`, `read`, `stage`, `publish` (`disk.ts:52-60`). Consumers see four business questions. Nothing upstream names a watcher event, a platform separator, or a stamp beyond `sameStamp`.

**Functional but not domain-functional:** "one directory, as the probe is allowed to see it" (`disk.ts:1`) is real functionality — yet it knows nothing about outlines, days, marks, or notes. `store` is generic over content (`surface` of `docs/architecture.md:67`: "carries no outline types"). Electricity for a house.

**Today's shrapnel path (the vault test):** every consumer of disk truth goes through this file. If the stage→publish protocol were inlined at each call site, a write gate change would throw across `store`, `ops`, and `server` — instead the two-verb split ("the split is the whole safety property," `disk.ts:28-33`) is one module's rule, changed in `disk.ts:180`'s watcher or nowhere. The grenade of "a `git pull` is one logical change and hundreds of events" (`store.test.ts:531`) lands in the settle-delay machinery, and the file is the wall.

Change count behind the socket: **3 edits since extraction**, and one of them is the `README`-scale documentation pass — the volatility is encapsulated, not erased.

### M2 — `packages/web/src/client/wire.ts`: the one connection

**The litany:** `ws:` vs `wss:` derived from `location.protocol` (`wire.ts:34-37`); the four transport states with the terminal one spelled out (`wire.ts:17-19`); the `retired` handler required by the seam "with no default, so a wire that compiles has been asked what happens when it dies" (`wire.ts:43-45`); the stale-tab handshake that "used to be an app's job" until juspay/kolu#2133 moved it into the framework (`wire.ts:23-28`); the top-level await that keeps "every consumer's import synchronous-looking" while the dial retries on its own fiber (`wire.ts:6-8`).

**The opaque socket:** `{ olai, connectionStatus }` (`wire.ts:57,64`). The whole client — 180+ modules — imports exactly this and nothing about a socket. One import of `connectSurface` (`wire.ts:32`) is the entire wire-facing surface of the app.

**Domain test:** "the one connection, and what it is doing" (`wire.ts:2`) is functionality at full voltage; it knows less about outlines than any module in the tree. Not the domain.

**Shrapnel:** the bug this file exists against is "a page that cannot say whether it is connected is a page that lies when it is not" (`wire.ts:14-15`) — a dead `documents.keys` rendering as an empty directory under a green light (`docs/architecture.md:29`). When that volatility lived across consumers, the shrapnel path was every subscription in every tab. Tossed into `wire.ts` plus `connection/status.ts`'s pure table (`status.ts:44`), it is a grenade in a one-file vault. 3 edits since `wire.ts` existed; the connective tissue has not had to move because the socket did not.

### M3 — `packages/chat/src/interpret.ts`: what the adapter MEANS

**The litany:** a `_meta.claudeCode.toolName` extension one adapter writes (`interpret.ts:106-113`); the Claude Code CLI's `mcp__<server>__<tool>` naming convention (`interpret.ts:75-88`); a `bypassPermissions` mode id that ACP leaves to the agent (`interpret.ts:43`); the plan-mode "Ready to code?" option that maps onto an allow-flavoured permit to `auto` — the trap "the panel approving its own permissions" (`interpret.ts:56-63`); a `system`/`init` message carrying the live model because the adapter's `configOptions` never see a `/model` switch (`interpret.ts:117-131`); a model picker whose `id === "model"` is "a bet of exactly the kind everything else here is" (`interpret.ts:166-173`). Adapter churn, every line of it.

**The opaque socket:** pure functions — `allowedWithoutAsking`, `toolNameIn`, `liveModelIn`, `modelPickerIn` — with the file's own constitution on line 15: "an agent that says none of this matches nothing here, and what happens then is that a person is asked. **Nothing is ever approved by failing to recognise it.**" That sentence is the receptacle's face: the losing direction is outward, to a human, never inward to a guess.

**Domain test:** adapter semantics are functionality and not domain — the outline does not care whether a permission request travelled via `_meta` or a config option (`interpret.ts:23-31` names the sibling `asks.ts` split: protocol asks vs adapter values).

**Shrapnel:** before #125, this volatility lived inside `agent.ts` (1033 lines, the file the kitchen-sinks debate named for exactly one cut). Finding the plan-mode trap would have meant reading the subprocess lifecycle to find a branch; "pointing olai at another one starts with a file rather than a search for the assumptions" (`interpret.ts:10-11`). The unit-testability alone is the oscilloscope retired: "the rule that stops this panel approving its own permissions is a function with unit tests rather than a branch reachable only by starting a subprocess" (`interpret.ts:18-21`).

This one shipped as the debate's ratified cut (`conclusion.md:12`, item 4) and I name it not to re-litigate but as the **type specimen** — the module receptacle, proven in the window since the last debate closed. It is what M1, M2, and M4 below are arguing from, not toward.

### M4 — `packages/server/src/edit.ts` + `packages/surface/src/edit.ts`: what a key MEANT

**The litany:** `Tab` says "indent this", not "reparent it under the node above and put it last"; `Ctrl+Enter` says "toggle done", not `set_done` or `clear_done` (`surface/src/edit.ts:15-19`). The five verbs of the `•••` menu and the two an undo replays (`place`, `remove`) ride the same union (`surface/src/edit.ts:38-60`); the placement arithmetic — "the row above," which parent an agent would have named for a mirror — is "a fact about the SET," read once, where the truth is (`server/src/edit.ts:12-17`); an id the caller named travels as-is so the keyboard cannot succeed where the tool refuses (`server/src/edit.ts:30-37`); an undo is a replayed *request* judged against the CURRENT set, "never a restore of an older one" (`server/src/edit.ts:39-45`, `web/src/client/edit/undo.ts:12-17`). Keyboard, menu, agent, undo: four spellings of volatility behind one closed `Edit` union and one `edit.apply` procedure.

**The opaque socket:** `surface/src/edit.ts` declares the union once — "adding one is an arm here and an arm in the resolver, and every other site is a compile error rather than a silent hole" (`surface/src/edit.ts:24-29`) — and `server/src/edit.ts` is its one resolver, pure over a `Reading` (line 21). The consumer is a keystroke; the socket is the union; the churn is everything about placement, mirrors, and inverses behind `edit.apply`.

**Domain test:** "what a keyboard — or a menu entry — MEANT" (`server/src/edit.ts:2`) is functionality, not the outline's business. The domain layer (`ops/plan.ts`) receives one settled `Request` per intent and learns nothing about keys (`surface/src/edit.ts:36`: "Ops itself learns none of this").

**Shrapnel:** without this vault, the HACKING rule "MCP and Web ops must be consistent; never deviate" (`server/src/edit.ts:24-25`) is enforced by hand across every key handler — the exact multi-file shrapnel pattern `editor-op-parity` was filed against (`debates/kitchen-sinks/conclusion.md:13`, in flight). The two faces (menu writes on the undo stack, `git log` #124) both resolve here, so the deviation is one file's compile error.

## What I sign at the other altitudes

The convergence is a ledger, so here are the entries I would sign at fable's and grok's, argued from the same grounding:

- **Package altitude (fable's): `@olai/store` to its own repo — but only as the *promotion* of M1's socket, on the precedent `@olai/git` already set.** `store` is generic over content, carries no outline types, and its `Disk`/`Probe`/`commit` triple is the most `@kolu/surface`-shaped thing olai owns: the probe/settle/backstop/write-gate sequence is exactly the "listener sequence" shape that `serveSurfaceApp` took (`docs/architecture.md:22,79`). The prior debate deferred it for a dated second consumer (`conclusion.md:18`); the module argument says the socket is already built and already disciplined — `packages/store/src/disk.ts` is edited less than any equivalently-load-bearing file — so the promotion, when a second consumer exists, will be a manifest laid on a socket rather than an extraction that has to invent one. I would sign that, with the date.

- **Upstream/graduation altitude (grok's): the *stale-tab + connection-status* handshake, already half-paid.** `wire.ts` and `status.ts` are olai-side faces over `system/identity` and `client.health()` that every `@kolu/surface` app re-derives: the four states, the fifth "partly live" state the transport cannot see (`status.ts:74-80`), the pill's rule that an indicator nobody renders is the bug. The consumers are "every kolu-surface app, not just olai" (grok's charter). I would sign paying `status.ts`'s pure table and `needsReload` into `@kolu/surface-app/solid` beside `connectSurface`, leaving olai's `wire.ts` a one-call composition — the second `serveSurfaceApp`.

## The close

The article's universal principle — the heart pumping blood with "high blood pressure and low pressure, salinity, viscosity, pulse rate," and the body still able to program ("Universal Principle") — does not describe a service registry. It describes an organ. A module *is* the organ: `disk.ts` is the heart of the store, `wire.ts` is the nerve of the client, `interpret.ts` is the reflex that never asks the brain, `server/edit.ts` is the hand's grip. A manifest is a jar the organ ships in when it goes to another body. Most of olai's organs are staying in this body. The vault is the file; the file's `export` list is the socket; the socket is the only thing a change may not break. Name the modules, keep the manifests honest, and the grenades land where they are thrown.
