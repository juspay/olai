# Round 2 — opencode: reply to fable and grok

Round 2's rule is to engage by name, steelman before rebutting, and concede
what is genuinely true. My round 1 made claims the other two have now
challenged with citations. The prosecutor's discipline is that a charge that
does not survive the evidence is withdrawn, not defended. Three of mine take
real damage; one is withdrawn outright. My central charge — that the doc's
*lift* section is hygiene dressed as volatility — survives, and grok's own
steel case sharpens rather than blunts it.

## I. Where I am beaten, and I withdraw

### (a) grok (§2, the `GitState` floor) beats my Section III

My round 1 said the `surface` mirror of `GitState` is "**forced by the
browser boundary**," because `server/runtime.ts:64` imports it from
`@olai/surface` and surface cannot import ops (`architecture.md:62`). I
stand by the narrow fact — the surface cannot import *ops'* `GitState`. But
I concluded "the mirror is **necessary**," and that conclusion is wrong, as
grok's alternative demonstrates. `GitState` can be declared *once* in
`format/src/committing.ts` as a Schema — a file whose own header
(`committing.ts:1-16`) already says "there is no git in this file" and which
already re-exports `RepoState` from `@olai/git/state` for exactly this
reason — and *both* ops and surface can then import it. `web` reads it
through surface, which depends legally on `format`. Nothing crosses the
browser boundary the wrong way; the layering my own round 1 cited
(`format ← surface ← server`) *permits* the very unification I called
impossible. I attacked the doc's *stated mechanism* ("extract the vocabulary
out of `pending.ts` and the mirror becomes an import") — that mechanism is
indeed broken, because it keeps `GitState` inside `ops` — and I took the
broken mechanism as proof the mirror was untouchable. Grok showed the real
move is to put the shape on the floor both sides already stand on. The bug
in the doc was the *home*, not the *goal*. Conceded: the mirror can die, by
a move I did not see, and my "layering-impossible" framing overclaimed.

What this costs me: my Section III's force was "the doc defeats itself with
its own layering." The accurate charge is narrower — the doc named the right
deduplication but hung it on the wrong file. Grok's framing ("the leftover
of #122") names the *axis* (#116/#119/#122 kept re-carving `GitState`'s
faces) that both the doc and I left un-named. fable (concession #3) already
half-concedes this: she prices the `pending.ts` vocabulary cut as possibly
weak, and says if it fails even the *module* bar she'll drop it to "fix the
duplication." Grok's floor proposal *is* the fix — and it is a better cut
than the doc's.

### (b) grok on the fake agent (§1 of his, II-c of mine)

My II-c had two legs, and I now think the first one is wrong. I argued the
fake agent's "generic half" cannot be node-builtins-only because line 74
imports `../support/ndjson.ts`. I have now read `ndjson.ts`. Its header is a
refutation of my own argument: *"the framing is all that is shared … this
file knows no method names,"* and it exists *because* — its words — the
fake agent *used to carry its own copy* of the six-line frame-splitter, and
"three copies … were the bug." So the import is not olai-test-support
knowledge leaking into a "generic" skeleton; it is the *framing* half of the
protocol, already extracted as a shared library in `support/`. The doc's
"node builtins alone" remains too strong as a literal claim, but the *substance*
— that the JSON-RPC framing half has no olai knowledge — survives the read
my round 1 claimed to do. My dependency-coupling leg stands, but only as: a
`chat/./testlib` lift must drag `ndjson.ts` along or re-duplicate it.
That is a *cost* of the lift, not the falsity I charged.

The leg grok actually broke is my second one, and he did it with better
evidence than the doc itself supplied. My position was "the unit tests belong
on `agent.ts` itself, reachable through `Questions`/`asks.ts`" — i.e. cut the
*decision function*, not the *skeleton*. Grok (§10, §2) agrees and goes
further, naming the pieces: `toolNameIn` reads `_meta.claudeCode.toolName`
(I re-verified: agent.ts ~1072-1091), `liveModelIn` reads the forwarded CLI
`init` message (~1048-1068), the `mcp__<server>__` prefix. All four are
*adapter interpretation*, and all four are pure readers sitting *inside* the
657-line session Manager. Cut those + the bypass decision *as functions* and
the permission-bypass rule is unit-testable *without* a fake subprocess at
all — which is the exact payoff the doc bought with an entire testlib
package, achieved with no new boundary. And grok's drift point is verified:
the fake agent's header still points at `packages/server/src/chat/agent.ts`,
a path that **no longer exists** (it is `packages/chat/src/agent.ts`). A
`./testlib` freeze would enshrine the illusion that the path is right. I
concede: extract the interpret functions; leave the skeleton until a second
consumer appears. My "inverted dependency" argument still stands against the
*package* shape, but grok's cut makes that package unnecessary sooner.

## II. What my round 1 got *right*, tested against both replies

I am not conceding my thesis, so here is what survives, and why neither reply
removes it.

**fable's own concession #1 is my thesis in her own words.** She concedes
the module-split table is "argued on co-location and legibility — a Hickey
argument, not a Lowy one," and waves this off with: "Lowy governs *published
boundaries*; a file split … invents no interface." That is a retreat, and
the topic document *itself* refuses the retreat — `topic.md:14` says the bar
"binds ALL three stances: **every** argument for or against a boundary
should name (or refute) the volatility axis," with no published/internal
carve-out. And the escape hatch leaks on Lowy's own terms: an internal
module that everything in the package imports *is* a stabilization point —
it is how you freeze a functional seam so that nobody can rewire it. The
dragons Lowy warns about ("over-engineered boundaries," "$variable is not
volatile") bite *harder* at module altitude, where there is no `bun install`
to machine-check the direction, than at package altitude. But I should
sharpen where fable is *right*: a truly *mechanical* extraction — moving
a pure, already-exported block to a sibling with no call-change — costs
nothing and invents nothing, so it needs no axis defence. On that standard
the honest split of the doc's Wave 0 is: `format/titles.ts` (already a
separately-exported `titleParts`, `derive.ts:715`) and `tests/selectors.ts` +
`budgets.ts` (module-scope constants, no fans) pass fable's test; `chat/reading.ts`
+ `rpc.ts` and `surface/git.ts` do *not* — the first two move things that
share the *same* ACP axis as the rest of `agent.ts` (grok's §1 point; moving
them is file reorganization, not encapsulation), and `surface/git.ts` splits
*a barrel* (`surface/index.ts:55-63` already defers chat to `chat.ts` and
keys to `edit.ts` — the barrel is the *declared* receptacle, and cutting it
into three sibling barrels is file furniture, not a socket).

**grok's §7 (markdown) confirms my II-b, better than I made it.** I said
`@olai/markdown` bundles two axes (rendering + token). Grok says the same
thing with a sharper edge: the *generic* pipeline is a pin (its doc-text
axis is mostly stable), and the *volatile* half — `/media` bijection,
picture allowlist, title unwrap, relative links — is precisely the two files
the lift would *leave behind* in `web`. So the package as proposed is
"the stable half, extracted." My Lowy-§6.5 "topic-bundle" verdict and grok's
"extractable ≠ should extract" are the same finding: the lift *selects for
stability*, which is the inverse of volatility decomposition. The socket's
supply-side volatility argument fable makes (§"The markdown pipeline" — GFM
footnotes, the anchor allowlist negotiation, the lowlight grammar swap,
media rewrite, all inside the pipeline with four callers on one plug) is the
*best* counter I have seen, and I credit it: those are real changes behind
one stable plug. But every one of them is a change in *what the olai format
renders* — format vocabulary, delivered to the client — not a change in the
generic `unified` orchestration. fable's evidence retro that the socket is
`*olai's rendering policy*`, and that socket is served fine by the
current *directory* inside `web`, because its only consumer *is* web. The
"server could someday share it (email digest)" — fable does not re-urge the
digest, so I take it as the doc's own speculative garnish, and Lowy's rule is
unambiguous: speculative volatility is not volatility. My verdict stands:
extract the axis (olai's render policy) as a unit *if and when* a second
consumer (the server digest) is on the roadmap and concrete; do not
pre-extract the generic pin.

**My II-a (precompress) — grok's §8 makes it stronger, and fable's §"Precompress" concedes the part that mattered.** I said the receptacle is
upstream and the file's own header (`precompress.ts:19-23`) points there.
grok adds a roadmap item I did not have: `precompress-upstream` (still
`todo`) states the plan is to *delete* `precompress.ts` when kolu's
`buildSurfaceClient` emits siblings. If the documented plan is deletion, an
olai leaf package "eventually its own repo" is the opposite receptacle.
fable (§"Precompress") calls its axis "weakest … in effect-size" and says the
doc only pairs it with the `precompress-dev-tax` fix "rather than urging it
alone." That is her pricing the volatility correctly and arriving at my
conclusion: fix the policy bug in place, do not mint a package for a file
headed to the recycler. `precompress-dev-tax` names the actual volatile axis
(skip rule, dist pruning, dev opt-out) — and none of those need a new package
to change. All three stances now agree; the remaining disagreement is only
whether the doc *meant* to urge it, which is a question about the doc, not
the code.

**grok's §6 (theme) half-concedes my II-b, and I him.** I said the theme
lift bundles two axes (token table + render policy). grok says theme is
"variable, not volatile": adding a row is already fully local (`palettes.ts`
header + `architecture.md:54` both say "adding a theme is adding a row"),
and the WCAG test holds each row, so no receptacle is missing. He and I
agree there is no axis justifying a *package*. He reads the same evidence
and calls the whole lift dead; I read it and note the theme dir has *already
grown a second axis inside itself* — `chrome.ts` (browser chrome painting),
whose header says "its own file because it is its own job" and names a
genuine second concern (iOS status bar) that the palette table does not
encapsulate. That is *activity* volatility (how a palette paints the *chrome*
vs the *page*) that the theme directory has already receptacl-ized
correctly *without* a package. So: no `@olai/theme`, and no need for one;
move `scale.ts` in beside `palettes.ts` (which grok and fable both endorse —
fable calls `scale.ts` "misfiled," it is the one misfiling all three of us
agree on), and the parcel is done. The doc's package case rests on "three
consumers across product boundaries," and two of those three (build.ts and
the tests) are *already* cross-package today through the testids/scale/clock
imports — meaning the consumers argument is really the stale-manifest
argument in costume, and grok is right to say so.

## III. Where grok's reframe is itself over-eager

Steelman worked both directions; grok's re-derivation has two places where
it commits the sin it prosecutes — substituting its own lens (change history)
for the volatility bar.

**grok's §9 (tool-prose drift) is a real finding but the fix is not a cut,
and the survey saw it first.** grok calls `tools.ts`'s restatements "Lowy's
duplicated volatility encapsulation — a first-class finding — marked 'not a
split.'" I verified: the `commit` description (`tools.ts:341-344`) restates
the whole-repo sweep, the selection-not-index rule, and the mid-merge refusal
that live in `pending.ts:26-45` and `plan.ts` — and the mark-tool
descriptions (`tools.ts:195-211`) restate the stamping/dating rules. That is
genuine drift risk, and the doc *did* see it (`kitchen-sinks.md:83`) — it
just refused to act. But grok's own prescription — "bind descriptions to the
decisions they restate, or generate them" — is not a receptacle; it is a
*deduplication mechanism*, and until somebody names the axis that makes
*prose* change independently of *policy*, binding them is shared *text*, and
shared text between two *reasons* (a log line vs a popover phrase, per
`said.ts`'s own recorded split justification) is the violation the opposite
direction. The correct Lowy reading is the one the doc's `said.ts` note
already gives: agent-facing prose and person-facing prose are *two different
axes* sharing policy inputs; the fix is to make each read from the policy
*source* (the planner's own `nudge`/`refusal` structures, already typed),
not to splice the two prose halves together. grok has the right target and
the wrong knife — a binding cannot be a receptacle, or it becomes the very
complected shared module Hickey would reject.

**grok's reframe has a voluntary blind spot where the doc's hygiene cuts are
free.** grok's §"Module cuts … I would not schedule" sweeps `reading.ts`/
`rpc.ts`, `tests/selectors.ts`+`budgets.ts`, `format/titles.ts` under "move
them if the scroll is painful / not Wave 0 of a program," and ranks the whole
doc's Wave 0 as "a cost ranking." But that is exactly what Wave 0 *is*, and
it is honest about it (`kitchen-sinks.md:91`: "free, no interfaces invented,
consumers already import by name"). If fable's altitude defence is good — and
I argued above it is, for *truly mechanical* moves — then `format/titles.ts`
(already one exported function) and the `world.ts` *module-scope* constants
(148 exportable names, no instance state — I counted) and `tests/selectors.ts`
+ `budgets.ts` cost *nothing*, invent nothing, and pay out in legibility.
grok is right that they are not Lowy findings — but nobody said they were;
Wave 0 does not claim to fix volatility, it claims to fix the survey's own
finding of misfiling, at zero risk. The stances converge here by accident:
grok wants the same files unmoved for *prioritization* reasons, I want them
moved for *hygiene* reasons at fable's *mechanical* bar, and fable wants them
as the doc wrote them. The honest synthesis is fable's altitude rule with
my correction: *mechanical = free, do it opportunistically; axis-bearing =
needs a named axis.* Under that rule `reading.ts`/`rpc.ts` fail (they share
the ACP axis with the rest of `agent.ts`, so moving them re-draws an axis
they don't own — grok is right there), while `titles.ts` passes.

## IV. The one thing all three of us are circling without saying

fable's retrodict (§"what I ask") is the strongest single argument in round 1
*from any stance*, and it is stronger than she deployed it. `@olai/git`'s
socket surviving three PRs unedited while everything above it churned is the
repo *telling us its axes*. Apply that same retrodict to the lift candidates
the doc actually urges and you get grok's table almost for free:

- `precompress.ts` — the plan is to *delete* it (roadmap). A receptacle you
  plan to remove is not a receptacle.
- generic `markdown/` — the volatile half stays in `web` either way (grok §7).
- `theme/` — already split itself once (chrome) at directory altitude.
- fake-agent skeleton — its own header has stale-drifted once already.

The four module/right-sized cuts that genuinely *do* name an axis emerge
from the same retrodict and sit *between* the doc and grok:

1. `GitState` to `format/committing.ts` (grok §2 — the #122 leftover; both
   ends import, the surface mirror dies). *All three stances can sign this.*
2. Adapter-interpretation functions out of `agent.ts` (grok §1 — `toolNameIn`
   /`liveModelIn`/`shouldBypass` as pure units; the real test enabler).
3. `scale.ts` beside `palettes.ts` (all three agree; misfiling, not package).
4. The two stale manifests (all three agree; honesty bugs, not refactors).

And the three *deferred* items the doc was right to defer — `@olai/committing`,
`@olai/store`, `inverseOf` — are deferred for the same reason: the driving
change has a name but no date. prove-then-extract governs *when*; none of
the three has proven *when*. That is not a failure of nerve; it is the bar
working as designed.

My prosecution in one sentence now: the doc's *instincts* (misfiling, drift,
honesty bugs) are largely right and its *package-lift* legal theory is largely
wrong — it argued from measurable isolation rather than named volatility, and
where it did name an axis it sometimes hung it on the wrong file (`pending.ts`
rather than the floor). grok's re-derivation fixes the home; fable's altitude
rule, corrected to apply only to *mechanical* moves, decides which of the
doc's Wave 0 survives. The decomposition the repo is asking for is grok's
four-item list plus the doc's hygiene corrections, and neither the doc's wave
plan nor my round-1 "hold everything" is what that yields.
