## Fable's challenge: retrodict `@olai/git`

> why did `@olai/git` (extracted along the "what git does" axis) survive contact with three subsequent PRs unchanged at its socket while `pending.ts` above it churned every time? If your axes are real, they should retrodict which past extractions aged well.

They do. That is the strongest question in either opening, and the answer is the reason I will not sign the survey's `pending.ts` vocabulary dump.

`@olai/git`'s socket is plumbing verbs: open this root, what state is the repository in, what has moved, what did HEAD have, commit exactly these paths, push. Those verbs did not change under `git-invisible` (#108), `one-git-indicator` (#119), `mcp-commit-manual` (#116), or `commit-whole-repo` (#122). What those PRs changed is **policy and presentation**:

- #108 added *news* (NoRepo vs Unusable) at the plumbing socket — and the `/state` subpath absorbed it. `pending.ts`'s `gitOf` / `whyOf` then *mapped* the new arms into faces and sentences (`pending.ts:123–184`). The leaf's verbs did not grow.
- #119 folded a second chip into one pill. That is `said.ts` and the header, not `Git.commit`.
- #116 made the MCP face speak the same `--commit` tri-state. That is `commits.ts` interpolating `commitDoors`, plus the waiting sentence, not a new git verb.
- #122 changed *what a commit names* (whole repository, two kinds of row, selection-not-index) and extracted the plumbing so the policy file could say so without owning `child_process`.

The extraction aged well because the axis was real: **plumbing vs policy**. Subsequent change hit the policy file. That is what a receptacle is *for*.

The survey's proposed `commits/vocabulary.ts` would have aged *badly* under the same PRs. It lumps `GitState` (wire), `COMMIT_MODES` (CLI, "never travel the wire", `pending.ts:84–86`), and `whyOf` / `commitDoor` (policy prose that #108 and #116 rewrote). Every one of those PRs would have opened the new file *and* the remaining closure. That is not a socket. That is a second place to remember the same policy.

Fable's own concession almost says this: the vocabulary extraction is "a coupling observation, not an axis," and the axis that *does* exist is "UX-copy churn … plus the `GitState` shape." Those are two axes. Copy churn lives in `whyOf` / `said.ts` / `commitDoors`. `GitState` is a published shape. Mixing them is the cut I refused.

## Opencode's GitState kill, and the floor it did not look at

Opencode's strongest finding is aimed at the survey's *bonus*, and as aimed it is correct.

The survey promised: extract vocabulary from `pending.ts` and surface's hand-kept `GitState` "becomes an import instead of a copy." Opencode traced it: `runtime.ts:64` imports `GitState` from `@olai/surface`; surface cannot import ops; web cannot import ops; the two declarations are a Schema that travels and an interface that is produced. **An `ops/commits/vocabulary.ts` cannot dissolve that mirror.** The survey's own layering forbids the import it advertised. Strike that bonus. I strike it too.

What opencode then concludes — "the duplication is **forced by the browser boundary**" — overstates the constraint. The browser boundary forbids surface importing *ops*. It does not forbid both of them importing the *floor*. That is already how this feature works.

`format/src/committing.ts:1–11` states the rule in so many words: this package is the floor both the ops layer and the wire spec stand on; a vocabulary spelled in either would have to be spelled again in the other; the ops layer produces, the surface carries, the browser draws. It already re-exports `RepoState` from `@olai/git/state` for that reason (`27–41`). `Pending`, `Writer`, `CommitRequest` already travel from format. Surface already imports them (`surface/src/index.ts:73–83`). `GitState` is the same kind of thing as `RepoState`: a published shape, four arms, no subprocess, decoded in a browser.

Put the Schema on the floor, next to `RepoState`. `gitOf` — a pure projection of `RepoState`, no git, no store, no Effect (`pending.ts:123–135`) — can sit beside `isReady` / `isPossible`, which are already there. Ops produces; surface carries the same Schema; the hand-kept mirror dies. No new package. No upward import. The survey's bonus is layering-impossible *from ops*. It is the existing pattern *from format*.

That is the leftover of #122, not a vocabulary dump. `COMMIT_MODES`, `whyOf`, `commitDoor`, `commitDoors` stay in ops. Opencode's (a) is also right on a smaller fact: `commitDoors` is not a dead export. `server/src/commits.ts:56` interpolates it into `--help`; `commits.test.ts:98` asserts the interpolation. The survey's "nothing in the file even calls" is true of `pending.ts` and false of the tree. Fable should strike the parenthetical.
