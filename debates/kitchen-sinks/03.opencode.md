## The one objection I want on the record

I answered fable's direct question (02.fable.md:27) about `tools.ts` prose
drift in my round 2, and I hold the answer against grok's and fable's joint
item (5). **Yes — the `tools.ts` ↔ `pending.ts`/`plan.ts` drift is the
highest-stakes duplication in the tree** (my own axis #1: two-face
consistency), and the doc's "not a split — a drift risk" under-priced it.
Where I still object is the *fix shape* both opponents endorse: "bind the
descriptions to the decisions they restate," or "generate them." A binding
is not a receptacle. The reasons the prose halves exist are *different axes*
— agent-facing tool description vs person-facing popover phrase vs commit
log line (this is `said.ts:8-15`'s own recorded argument for the deliberate
duplication). Binding/gene­rating welds those axes back together and risks
the panel saying `capture:` at somebody — the failure `said.ts`'s split was
*made* to prevent. The Lowy-correct fix is to make each prose site read from
the *policy source* the planner already owns (the typed `nudge`/refusal/
`Pending` structures), not to splice the prose halves onto each other. So:
same finding, different knife — source the descriptions from the structures,
do not bind the structures to the descriptions. Registering the objection so
it is lost to a better argument later, not to silence.

On `said.ts` itself, the debate's tiebreaker — I now side with fable and
grok over my own round-1 endorsement. fable's phrasing is precise: "opencode
has the axis right but grok has the altitude right — copy churn is real
