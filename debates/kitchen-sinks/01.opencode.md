# Round 1 — opencode: the prosecution on Lowy's bar

The topic names my burden: presume most of these cuts are *functional
decomposition in volatility clothing*, steelman each before cutting it, and
hold every argument — for or against — to the electricity-receptacle bar: a
boundary must encapsulate a **named axis of change (likelihood × effect)**,
not merely group related functionality. "Variable is not volatile."

I read the proposal against the code, not the summary. It is a careful,
honest document — more honest with itself than most refactor surveys — and it
already concedes several of the cuts I would have forced. But it does not
apply Lowy's bar to its own headline cuts; it applies a *hygiene* bar
(length, misfiling, stale manifests) and then reaches for volatility
vocabulary to legitimize them. Those are not the same test. Where the
proposal's own evidence is checked line-by-line, three of its cuts fail it
on the facts, one is pointed at the wrong receptacle, and its two best moves
are understated.

First, the steelman I owe — because the proposal earns more of its case than
my stance presumes.

## I. What the proposal gets right, on the volatility axis from the start

Concede before rebutting, and concede the *strong* version.

**The `@olai/git` precedent is real and the doc names it the right way.**
`packages/git/package.json`'s manifest says it plainly: *"`effect` and
nothing else, deliberately: this is a LEAF … a package that shells out to
git and knows nothing about outlines, writers or a wire."* That is Lowy's
socket with a named supply side: *which git, and whether it can answer.*
That axis has demonstrably changed (NoRepo vs. Unusable split under #108) and
is on the roadmap (the `precompress-dev-tax` work). The extraction was
correct because the receptacle is stable under its own volatility — the
`/state` subpath exists precisely so the wire half can travel without the
subprocess half.

**The module-level split of `chat/src/agent.ts` is the proposal's best
move — because it names a sequence/activity axis, not a length.** The file's
own header (`agent.ts:27-34`) states the rule: *"which permission requests
are answered without asking"* — i.e. the bypass decision is the one thing
this closure decides that is not hostage to the subprocess. The body of that
rule (`agent.ts:341-364`) is the load-bearing `onPermission`: it must find
the tool, check `ours`, and *not* auto-pick the first allow, because the
adapter maps plan mode's "ready to code?" onto a permission whose first
allow-flavoured option silently switches the session to `auto`. That is a
named, safety-critical axis — *what olai auto-approves* — with likelihood
(the adapter's plan-mode trap is real) and effect (grant the agent a decision
nobody made). Cutting `permit.ts` out so that rule is a pure decision
function is not file-length management; it is putting a socket in front of
the one thing in the room that can hurt somebody. The same argument earns
`model.ts` (the three-variable `pickedModel`/`liveModel`/`labels` state at
`agent.ts:474-478` that nothing else reads) and `rpc.ts`/`reading.ts` — but
on the *axis* ("payload reading," "permission policy"), not on line count.
Here the proposal and I agree; the difference is I would not have called it
row #2 of a length-ranked table.

**The `said.ts` split is earned on a real axis — presentation vocabulary.**
Six copy tables (`MARK`, `DETAIL`, `SAID`, `GLYPH`, `HOW`, `HOW_TONE`,
`WHO`, `BLOCKED`) ride one state machine. The axis is *how a fact about git
is phrased to a person* — that is what changes (the human files
`one-git-indicator`; copy changes; tones change) with high likelihood and
low blast radius, because the state machine's logic is stable while the
words are not. And `said.ts:8-15` already documents the deliberate
duplication of the ops layer's message table on exactly these grounds — "one
of them is a log line somebody greps years later and the other is a phrase
in a popover" — which is Lowy's own point that the *same* change should not
have to be made in two places for two different reasons. The split makes
each reason one file. Correct cut, and the doc is right that it does not
disturb the recorded duplication decision.

**The two stale-manifest fixes are not refactors at all — they are honesty
bugs, and the doc is right to flag them "regardless of everything above."**
`packages/web/package.json`'s `//exports` claims the *one* cross-boundary
import is `src/client/testids.ts`; the code says four (`testids.ts`,
`clock.ts`, `theme/css.ts`, `edit/draft.ts` — confirmed by grep across
`packages/tests/step_definitions/`). `packages/tests/package.json`'s
`//dependencies` claims three and imports the same four. I verified this.
Fix these, and not as part of a wave — as a one-line-each correctness patch.
This is the strongest single paragraph in the document, because it is the
only claim that is both *true today* and *free*.

**"Not sinks" is the section I would defend hardest.** The doc's own list
(`kitchen-sinks.md:85-87`) — `plan.ts`'s thirteen `planX`, `server/edit.ts`,
`format/validate.ts`, `chat/asks.ts`, `server/serve.ts` — is a Lowy
evaluation done well: each says *why length is not mixture* for that file.
I checked the two I was most likely to attack. `validate.ts` keeps the
"rules checked in exactly one place" invariant — the file *is* the
receptacle for format-validity volatility, and splitting it would duplicate
the encapsulation. `server/serve.ts` is a composition root, i.e. the one
place sequence volatility *belongs* (Lowy: orchestrators hold sequence
volatility; a composition root is an orchestrator). The doc reads these
correctly.

That is a real foundation. Now the prosecution.

## II. Where the doc fails its own bar — the three cuts that don't survive a read

My stance says a debater who checks `file:line` beats one who argues from
the summary. I checked. Three of the proposal's headline claims are not
supported by their own citations.

### (a) `precompress.ts` — the axis is named, but it is *kolu's* axis, and the receptacle already exists upstream

This is the most instructive failure because it is the closest miss. The
proposal (`kitchen-sinks.md:25-29`) sells `precompress.ts` as "Zero olai
knowledge … a standalone 'precompress a static dir' utility that happens to
live inside a SolidJS package." So, lift it to a leaf, then to its own repo.

Read the file. Its header (`precompress.ts:1-23`) is a precise description
of a **kolu** contract, not an olai one:

> *"The static layer in `@kolu/surface-app` already negotiates them …
> Negotiation is scoped to `/assets/*` only … Upstream shape worth knowing
> (flag in the PR, do not patch the Nix-store package): `buildSurfaceClient`
> does not emit siblings itself, so every surface-app consumer re-derives
> this post-step — a natural home for a `precompress?: boolean` on that
> helper when someone next touches kolu."*

The volatility axis here is genuine: *content-encoding policy for static
assets* changes (brotli today, zstd tomorrow; the `/assets/*` scoping;
kolu#1319). Likelihood real, effect contained. That is exactly the kind of
axis Lowy wants behind a receptacle. **But the receptacle is
`@kolu/surface-app`, and the file says so.** The proposal extracts this into
an *olai* leaf, which is the duplicated-encapsulation finding fired in the
Lowy framework's own words — "a parallel receptacle for an already-
encapsulated axis is duplicated volatility encapsulation." Every
`surface-app` consumer re-derives this post-step *because the primitive
lives one level too high in olai rather than one level too low in kolu.*
The correct move, which the file itself names, is a `precompress?: boolean`
on `buildSurfaceClient` in kolu — pay it upstream, like `listener.ts` was
(architecture.md:79 documents that exact precedent: *"Paid upstream …
`serveSurfaceApp` … two copies kept in step by hand"*). The proposal lifts
the copy down instead of deleting it up. And its supporting fact is wrong:
`server/src/precompress.test.ts` does **not** import the util — it
hand-rolls `brotliCompressSync`/`gzipSync` (I read it; lines 14-16, 30-39)
to assert end-to-end negotiation. The "server already reaches across to test
it" claim is false, and with it goes half the "this wants to be shared"
evidence. This cut is a graduation candidate, not a package lift — and
prove-then-extract governs *when*, which here is "when kolu next touches
`buildSurfaceClient`."

### (b) The markdown pipeline lift — a section split presented as a package, and the section never named

The proposal (`kitchen-sinks.md:11-17`) wants `@olai/markdown`. The surface
argument is good: `web/src/client/markdown/` is self-contained except two
files, and I verified the two — `rewrite.ts:37-42` reaches
`@olai/format.pictureOf`, `@olai/surface.mediaHref`, `../testids.ts`;
`tags.ts:23-24` reaches `@olai/format.titleParts` and `../testids.ts`. So:
inject the two olai plugins, lift the generic pipeline.

This is the cut that most needs Lowy's §6.5 (package coherence) and does not
get it. Read the package as a new consumer: what is it? "A remark/rehype
pipeline with two plugins left behind and a third (`scale.ts`) that is not
markdown at all." The proposal itself confesses the incoherence — entry 1
ends by saying `scale.ts` (334 lines, zero imports, a *design-token table
plus CSS emitter*, consumed by `build.ts:36` and `build.ts:100`) "belongs
with the theme." So the "one big liftable subsystem" is actually **two
different axes wearing one directory**: a *rendering* axis (markdown →
sanitized HTML, changes when the format's markdown interpretation changes)
and a *token* axis (the visual scale, changes when the theme changes —
`scale.ts`'s own header is a design-systems argument about GitHub's
stylesheet and WCAG, i.e. aesthetic volatility). Lowy's bar: one volatile
axis per receptacle. This is two. A package named `@olai/markdown` that
secretly needs its scale table moved out first is not one socket; it is the
"topic-bundle" §6.5 warns about. The correct cut is the two axes separately
— and the scale half is already subsumed by the theme cut the doc makes next
door. As proposed, the lift bundles an axis it does not own. That is
functional decomposition (a directory of plumbing) in volatility clothing.

### (c) The fake-ACP-agent skeleton lift — the "generic half" has a hidden olai import, and the axis is the client, not the server

The proposal (`kitchen-sinks.md:35-39`) splits `tests/agent/fake-acp-agent.ts`
into a "generic half" (JSON-RPC framing, session lifecycle, the stdin loop)
that "depends on **node builtins alone** (no SDK, no effect, no workspace
import)" and an olai scenario-script half, and lifts the generic half to
`@olai/chat`'s `./testlib`.

The factual premise is false. Line 74: `import { readMessages } from
"../support/ndjson.ts"`. The "generic, zero-workspace" half imports olai's
own test support. That import is not incidental — the fake agent reads the
same newline-delimited JSON framing the real suite asserts on, which is the
whole point of a *fake* (it must be indistinguishable from the real thing
*as far as the suite can tell*). So the clean generic/olai split the lift
requires does not exist: the framing is already shared with the test world
it is supposed to be independent of.

And the volatility argument points the wrong way. The proposal's stated
payoff — "chat's most safety-critical logic … is today reachable **only**
through Cucumber spawning a real subprocess; a testlib skeleton makes those
unit tests" — names the axis precisely: *the ACP client contract* (what a
session lifecycle looks like, what a prompt/cancel exchange is). But that is
volatility in the **client** of the agent, i.e. `@olai/chat`'s own
`agent.ts` — the thing that consumes the protocol. The fake agent is the
*server* of that protocol. Lifting the server skeleton into `chat`'s
`./testlib` inverts the dependency that makes `@olai/git`'s `./testlib` and
`@olai/log`'s `./testlib` work: those are fixtures a package publishes *for
consumers above to test against* (see their `//exports` comments — "published
so packages ABOVE can write their fixtures the same way"). `chat` publishing
a fake *agent* for *itself* is not that shape; there is no package above
chat that needs to fake an ACP agent, and the manifest-convention precedent
the doc cites (`format`, `log`, `git`, `ops`) is the opposite direction.
The safety-critical unit tests the doc wants are real and worth having, but
they are tests **of `agent.ts`** against a stub — which the existing
`Questions`/`asks.ts` modules already allow in-process — not a reason to
lift the fake. The stdin-on-import hazard the file's own header records
(`fake-acp-agent.ts:65-67`: it lives in `agent/` *because* importing it
reads stdin) is a real smell, but the fix is a `bin/` wrapper for the file
as it stands, not a package.

## III. The cut the doc aims at the wrong receptacle

**`surface/src/index.ts` → `git.ts`, `outlines.ts`.** The doc
(`kitchen-sinks.md:65`) flags the 500-line `index.ts` as "spec + barrel +
three subjects that should be siblings." There is a real misfiling: the
`GitState` schema at `surface/index.ts:212-218` duplicates, structurally,
the `GitState` interface in `ops/pending.ts:107-112`. The doc's bonus claim
is the one I want to kill precisely, because it misdiagnoses *why* the
mirror exists.

`kitchen-sinks.md:80`: *"`GitState` shaped twice … one owner after the
vocabulary extraction."* This is the pivotal claim — extract the vocabulary
from `pending.ts` and the surface's hand-kept mirror "becomes an import
instead of a copy."

**It cannot.** I traced it. `server/src/runtime.ts:64` imports `GitState`
from `@olai/surface`, and `surface/index.ts:207-210` says *why* the two are
kept separate: *"The shape is deliberately the same as `@olai/ops`'s own
`GitState` … the server hands one straight to the other, so the two
declarations drifting is a type error at the seam rather than a mapping
nobody re-reads."* The surface's `GitState` is a `Schema.Struct` because it
**travels the wire** and must decode in a browser; ops's is a plain
interface produced server-side. The browser half can never import the ops
half — that edge is the whole reason `web` does not depend on `ops`
(architecture.md:62: "`web` does not depend on `server` … they share a wire
contract, `surface`, not an import"; the same rule bars `web` from `ops`).
The duplication is **forced by the browser boundary**, not by the missing
extraction. Extracting `pending.ts`'s vocabulary into a new
`commits/vocabulary.ts` (doc's wave-1 move) leaves the surface's Schema
exactly where it is, because the surface is the *receptacle for the wire
axis* and cannot take a dependency on the orchestration layer above it.
The doc's own dependency direction (`format ← surface ← server`) forbids
the very import it promises. This is the proposal defeating itself with its
own layering argument — the strongest single finding I have, because the doc
states the principle (`kitchen-sinks.md:7`, "a leaf … knowing nothing above
it") and then violates it to make cut #1's bonus look free.

## IV. What is actually volatile in this repo — the axis the doc's file-size lens misses

My stance requires me to say what *would* survive the bar, not only what
fails it. Here the proposal's own strongest instinct — its §"what is NOT
lifting" and "checked, and explicitly not sinks" sections — is pointing at
the right principle, and I want to push it one step further than the doc
takes it.

The repo's demonstrated, roadmap-bearing volatility axes, from the git
history and `HACKING.md`, are:

1. **The two write faces must not diverge** — `olai web` vs. `olai mcp`.
   This is HACKING.md's MCP rule, it is the *highest-likelihood ×
   highest-effect* axis in the system, and the recent history is a string of
   its consequences (`commit-whole-repo`, `editor-op-parity`, `one-git-indicator`,
   `mcp-commit-manual`). The receptacle that encapsulates it already exists:
   it is the *shared ops table and `gitWiring`* (`runtime.ts:163-173`,
   "`gitWiring` … ONE spelling, because there are two composition roots …
   HACKING.md's rule is that they must not diverge"). This is the axis the
   codebase is actually organized around, and it is a *sequence* axis —
   the workflow of a write — with the ops layer as its orchestrator.

2. **The ACP adapter's behavior** — plan mode, elicitation, permission
   mapping. Named above; the `permit.ts` cut is the right receptacle.

3. **The wire / view contract** — what travels and how it is drawn. The
   `said.ts` split and the theme/scale re-homing are the right receptacles.

4. **Git plumbing** — already encapsulated in `@olai/git`.

Now the finding the doc's lens produces and misses. The proposal is organized
as a list of *files ranked by length* (`kitchen-sinks.md:57-68`, "| # | file
| lines |"). But Lowy's framework says the unit of decomposition is the
**axis**, and the axes above do not line up with the fat files. The two
fattest, most-touched files in the repo right now — `ops/pending.ts` (987
lines) and `ops/plan.ts` (1708 lines) — are fat precisely because they sit
on the *#33 axis* (whole-repo commit semantics, selection-not-index,
two-kinds-of-row), which is the axis the `commit-whole-repo` (#122) work
just finished re-carving and the `editor-op-parity` work is about to re-carve
again. The proposal's `pending.ts` vocabulary pull (wave 1) is, I concede,
a legitimate cut of *that* axis — `COMMIT_MODES`/`gitOf`/`whyOf` are pure
vocabulary, format-types-only, read by server — **but** (a) its premise that
`commitDoors` "nothing in the file even calls" is wrong (it is called from
`server/src/commits.ts:56` and `commits.test.ts:98`; the doc read its own
file incorrectly), and more importantly (b) pulling vocabulary does not touch
the 800 lines of `survey`/`commit`/`push` orchestration that is the actual
volatility, and the doc *correctly says do not split that* — which is an
admission that the file's size is mostly one volatile axis, properly
co-located. Length is not the signal. `plan.ts` (doc: "long, not mixed,"
stowaways aside) is the same lesson at double the line count, and the doc
reads it correctly.

So the reframe I would put to grok and fable: the doc's *method* (three
sweeps by package and by file length) is a *Hickey* method — where are
concerns interleaved? — and it found real interleavings. But its *rhetoric*
(py package lifts, wave sequencing, "the one big client subsystem that is
genuinely liftable") keeps reaching for *Lowy* legitimacy the method did not
earn. Of the four package lifts it urges, one (`precompress`) points at an
upstream receptacle it should pay instead of copy, one (`@olai/markdown`)
bundles two axes into a bag, one (`fake-agent` skeleton) has a false premise
and an inverted dependency — and only the markdown *section split* and the
scale/theme re-homing survive, both of which are *moves within* existing
receptacles, not new packages. The package count in this tree is low
*because the layering is honest*; the doc's own first paragraph says so
("the *package* layering is not the problem"). Its package-lift section
argues against its own verdict.

## V. Disposition

Not a vote, so: what I would actually do, in order.

1. **Fix the two stale manifests** — `"regardless of everything above,"` and
   the only item that is an unqualified bug today. (`web: //exports`;
   `tests: //dependencies` — four real imports, not one/three.)
2. **Do the Wave-0 module moves** — `chat/reading.ts`+`rpc.ts`,
   `tests/selectors.ts`+`budgets.ts`, `format/titles.ts`, `surface/git.ts`,
   the `scale.ts` re-homing. Free, mechanical, honest.
3. **Do the `said.ts` and `agent.ts` (permit/model) splits** — but file them
   under their *axes* (presentation vocabulary; permission policy), and drop
   the length-ranked table, because the ranking is the part of the doc that
   is wrong.
4. **Hold the package lifts.** `@olai/markdown` until the scale half is out
   and what remains is one axis; `precompress` until it is paid into kolu's
   `buildSurfaceClient` (the file names the PR); the fake-agent skeleton
   until its `support/ndjson.ts` coupling is broken by a `bin/` wrapper
   rather than laundered through a `./testlib`.
5. **Kill the `GitState` mirror bonus** — the extraction may still be worth
   doing for its own axis, but the "one owner after the vocabulary
   extraction" promise is layering-impossible and should be struck from the
   doc before someone builds on it.

The steelman is that this is one survey, three full sweeps, real line-counts,
real import traces, and an honest "not sinks" list. The prosecution is that
it measured the wrong thing cleanly and reached for the wrong frame to sell
it. Boundaries here should be carved where the change lives: the two-face
consistency axis, the ACP axis, the wire/view axis, the git axis already
carved. The file-size lens mostly rediscovered those four and then measured
their shadows.
